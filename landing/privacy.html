Privacy Policy

DropPin Vault  •  Effective August 9, 2026  •  Developer: Erik Brown

DropPin Vault ("the App") is built around a simple idea: the places you save belong on your device, not on someone else's server. This policy explains exactly what the App does and doesn't do with your information, in plain language.

The short version: Your saved spots, photos, tags, and notes are stored only in the App's local database on your phone. Nothing about them is uploaded anywhere — unless you personally choose to turn on Google Drive backup, in which case your own backup file goes straight to a private, hidden folder in your own Google Drive account that only you (and the App, on your behalf) can access. We never see it.

1. Who We Are

DropPin Vault is developed by Erik Brown, an independent developer. There is no company, server infrastructure, or backend database operated on your behalf — the App is a self-contained piece of software that runs entirely on your device.

2. Information Stored Locally on Your Device

The core function of the App — saving locations, photos, notes, tags, and vehicle information — is handled entirely by a local database and local file storage on your phone. This data:

  • Never leaves your device automatically
  • Is never transmitted to Erik Brown or to any server we operate (because none exists)
  • Is never sold, rented, or shared with advertisers
  • Is fully under your control — you can view, edit, export, or permanently delete it at any time from within the App

This includes: saved location coordinates and addresses, photos you attach to a spot, notes and tags, vehicle names/colors/notes, and your App preferences (theme, default settings, and similar).

3. Optional Google Drive Backup

This is the one feature in the App that involves a third party, so we want to be precise about it.

If — and only if — you explicitly choose to connect Google Drive from onboarding or Settings, the App requests access to a single, narrow Google API scope:

https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.appdata

This scope grants access to nothing but a hidden, application-specific storage folder ("appDataFolder") inside your own Google Drive account. It is invisible in your normal Drive file browser, cannot be seen or opened by any other app, and does not grant access to any other file in your Drive.

When Drive backup is on, the App periodically packages your Vault data — the same local database contents and photos described above — into a single backup file, and uploads it directly from your device to that hidden folder in your own Google Drive account. When you restore (typically shown once, on first launch, if a backup already exists), the App downloads that same file directly back to your device.

At no point does this data pass through, get stored on, or become accessible to any server operated by Erik Brown. The transfer happens directly between your device and Google's Drive servers, authenticated as you, using credentials only you control. We do not have a copy. We cannot read it. We cannot recover it for you if you lose access to your Google account — only you and Google can do that.

You can disconnect Google Drive backup at any time from Settings → Data → Google Drive Backup. Disconnecting stops future backups; it does not delete the backup file already sitting in your Drive — you can remove that yourself from your Google Account's storage management, or the App's Disconnect confirmation will remind you of this.

4. Signing In With Google

Connecting Google Drive requires signing in with a Google account, using Google's own sign-in system (Android Credential Manager). This shares your basic Google account identity (your email address) with the App so it can show you which account is connected. That email is stored only in the App's local preferences on your device, purely for display — it is not sent anywhere by us.

5. Location Data

The App uses your device's location services to let you save where you are and navigate back to saved spots. Location processing for saving, navigating, and organizing spots happens on your device.

One exception: to turn coordinates into a readable address (e.g. "123 Main St" instead of raw latitude/longitude), the App uses Android's built-in geocoding service, which — depending on your device — may send the coordinates to Google's geocoding servers to resolve an address. This is standard Android system behavior used by most location-aware apps, is governed by Google's own Privacy Policy, and is not something we collect, log, or have access to ourselves.

6. Camera & Photos

Photos you take or attach to a spot are saved to local storage on your device and referenced by the local database. They are never uploaded anywhere unless you've turned on Google Drive backup (see Section 3), in which case they travel the same direct-to-your-own-Drive path as everything else.

7. On-Device Text Recognition

The App can read text from photos (for example, parking garage level signs) using on-device text recognition. This processing happens entirely on your device using a bundled recognition model — no image or extracted text is sent to any server for this feature.

8. Automatic Diagnostics & Usage Data

To keep the App stable, it includes Google Firebase Crashlytics and Firebase Analytics. In the interest of full transparency:

  • Crashlytics automatically collects crash reports if the App unexpectedly stops working — this can include a technical stack trace, your device model, OS version, and App version, sent to Google's Firebase servers so the underlying bug can be fixed.
  • Analytics automatically collects basic, aggregate usage signals (such as app opens and session length) to understand how the App is used overall.

Neither of these tools is used for advertising. The App explicitly disables Android's advertising ID and Google's ad-attribution permissions, so this data cannot be linked to you for ad targeting, and we don't use it for that purpose. This diagnostic data is processed by Google under Firebase's own privacy terms, not collected or stored by us directly.

9. What We Don't Do

  • We don't operate a server that stores your Vault data.
  • We don't sell, rent, or share your data with advertisers or data brokers.
  • We don't show ads or use advertising identifiers.
  • We don't require an account to use the App's core features — Google sign-in is only ever needed for the optional Drive backup.
